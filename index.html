§ 02The title

Buyers search "Auto." Not "Autograph."

Miss the token a buyer actually types and your card doesn't appear in the search that would have sold it. Every card app writes a title. ListRight writes it from rules — the same value tokens, in the hobby's order, inside the 80-character limit, every single time.

/250Print run. The denominator is the information — a /25 is a different card from a /250. Always in the title.
RCRookie card. Only when the card actually carries the RC mark. A 1st Bowman prospect is not a rookie — a mistake that costs money in both directions.
1st BowmanFirst Bowman. Its own token, placed before the brand so it reads "1st Bowman Chrome."
PSA 10Grade. The single strongest value token on a slab, and it leads the title.
1/1One of one. A different class of object from a /250 — and never styled like one.
SIRSpecial Illustration Rare. Two gold stars, not one. Resolving SIR vs IR before you list is the difference between two very different prices.
§ 03The price

Priced off sold comps.
Not a "market price."

What everyone else does

Card tools price off a market feed — TCGplayer, or their own database. It's a number, and you're asked to trust it.

What ListRight does

Reads what the card actually sold for on eBay. Strips lots, separates graded from raw, drops outliers, and gives you a median with a range — then shows you every comp behind it.

Including the ones it threw out. That hollow row in the example above — struck price, "wrong parallel" — is a comp ListRight refused to price on. No other tool shows you its own rejects.

§ 04What runs on every card

Seven stages.
No guessing at any of them.

This is the whole pipeline. Nothing here is a language model being asked politely to produce a good title — the identification is structured, and everything after it is deterministic code with tests.

  1. 01 · ReadFront and back. HEIC and JPEG, EXIF rotation corrected, decompression-bomb guarded, downscaled to 1600px before anything sees it. The back resolves year, set and serial the front can't.
  2. 02 · IdentifyStructured extraction, not prose. Twenty fields come back typed — sport, year, brand, set, player, team, card number, parallel, insert, serial, print run, grader, grade, cert — each with a confidence. The model fills a schema; it doesn't write a sentence and hope.
  3. 03 · TokensRules override the model. A legible serial derives the print run whether the card reads 074/250, /250 or 250. A 1st Bowman prospect is forced to not be a rookie, because it isn't one — a distinction most tools get wrong in both directions.
  4. 04 · Title & descriptionAn 80-character budget, spent by priority — plus the description. Grade, player and year are never dropped. Then RC, 1st Bowman and the print run — short, high-signal — outrank the long brand string and the card number. Duplicate variety text is removed when the brand already contains it, and ALL-CAPS grader fields are re-cased without breaking RC, PSA, SP or SSP. The description is written alongside it, with population context and no external links — eBay treats those as a policy violation.
  5. 05 · CompsReal eBay sold listings — 15M+ of them, including accepted Best Offer prices that eBay hides from you. That's the truest record of what a card actually trades for, and most tools never see it.
  6. 06 · SegmentThe step that decides whether the number means anything. Multi-card lots stripped out. Graded separated from raw. Serial-numbered parallels matched to serial-numbered parallels. Currencies split. Stale sales aged out. A comp set that skips this is just an average of unrelated cards.
  7. 07 · PriceMedian, not mean — one shill sale can't drag it. Range is the interquartile band, so the middle half of real sales. Confidence is computed from sample size and spread, and it's always shown with the numbers behind it, never as a bare adjective.

Graded slabs additionally verify against PSA's public API — identity and population, by cert number.
